Problem Statement 1: Enhancing Patient Monitoring and Care Through Visual Context Understanding
Context
In hospitals, elder-care facilities, and home-based care settings, patient conditions are often monitored visually through cameras or periodic image capture (e.g., posture, wounds, mobility, medical device placement). However, interpreting these visual observations and translating them into meaningful clinical insights requires continuous human supervision, which is not always feasible.
Current monitoring systems focus either on raw visuals or numerical data, but fail to combine visual observations with contextual understanding such as patient instructions, care plans, or symptom descriptions.
Problem
There is no effective mechanism to contextually interpret visual patient data together with natural language information (such as care notes or patient complaints) to support caregivers in identifying potential issues early.




Problem Statement 2: Improving Accessibility of Learning Content for Diverse Learners
Context
Educational content is often delivered through visually rich materials such as slides, diagrams, charts, laboratory setups, and classroom demonstrations. Students with visual impairments, learning difficulties, or language barriers frequently struggle to interpret such content without additional support.
While accessibility accommodations exist, they are usually manual, inconsistent, or limited in scope, placing additional burden on educators and institutions.
Problem
There is no scalable mechanism that can interpret visual educational content and translate it into clear, accessible language tailored to different learner needs, leading to unequal learning experiences.
